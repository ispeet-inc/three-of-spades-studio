## How to Play: Three of Spades

## Game Flow Overview

1. **Deal** the cards – 10 to each player.
2. **Bid** for the contract – highest bidder aims to fulfill their declared points with a hidden partner.
3. **Choose** trump suit and a hidden teammate card.
4. **Play** all tricks, following suit and using trump.
5. **Reveal** teams when the teammate card appears.
6. **Score** the round and adjust for bids met or missed.

------

## Game Setup

- **Players:** 4 (sitting in a circle, teams will be hidden at first)
- **Deck:** 40 cards (all suits, typically 10-Ace in each; remove twos, threes except 3♠, fours, sixes, eights, nines if necessary)
- **Deal:** 10 cards per player. The dealer rotates clockwise each hand.

------

## Bidding Phase

- **Starting Bid:** 165 points
- **How Bidding Works:**
  - Each player may bid or pass, increasing by at least 5 points per bid.
  - The highest bidder becomes the “bidder” (contractor).
  - All pass? Redeal (alternatively, lowest allowed bid may win—set your rule).
- **Bidder’s Task:** Their team must win at least as many points as their bid this round.

------

## Trump & Teammate Selection

- **Trump Selection:** The bidder picks any one suit as the trump for that hand.
- **Teammate Card:** The bidder selects a card (not in their own hand) as the “teammate card.”
  - The player who holds this card becomes the secret partner of the bidder.
  - (Optional – add: “3 of Spades or trump suit can/can’t be chosen as teammate card” if there are restrictions.)
- **Teams:** Secret at first – only revealed when the teammate card is actually played in a trick.

------

## Playing Phase (Trick-Taking)

- **First Trick:** The bidder leads the very first card.
- **Following Suit:** Each player must play a card of the suit led if possible. If not, any card—including trump—may be played.
- **Trump:** Trump suit cards beat all other suits.
- **Winning a Trick:** Highest trump wins the trick, or if no trump is played, highest card of the suit led wins.
- **Trick Winner:** Collects the trick and leads next.
- **Teammate Reveal:** When the teammate card is played, teams are revealed and from then on, partners can openly work together.

## Example Trick

- Suit led: Diamonds. Player A plays King♦, Player B plays 3♠, Player C (no diamonds) plays Jack♥ (trump), Player D plays 10♦.
- Trick goes to Jack♥ if hearts are trump; otherwise, King♦ wins unless 3♠ is scored for its value.

## Scoring & End of Round

- At the end of each hand, add up each team’s trick points using the scoring table above.
- If the bidding team (bidder + secret partner) meets or exceeds the contract, they keep their points.
- If they fail, the defending team receives all points for the round (or you may subtract the bid from the bidder’s total—specify your house rule here).
- Play continues for a set number of hands, or you may declare the first team to reach a target as the overall winner.

## Special/House Rules & FAQ

- **What if the Teammate Card is never played?**: Teams remain secret; at round’s end, points are still divided per intended team arrangement.
- **What if all pass on the first bid?**: Redeal the cards (or award lowest starting bid).
- **Can the 3 of Spades be a teammate card or trump?**: (Clarify your rule—typically, any card not held by the bidder is allowed.)
- **What if there’s a misdeal?**: Redeal.
- **Endgame:** Specify—one hand, best of X, or first team to X points wins the whole game.
- **Beginner tip:** Always try to protect your high-value cards, especially the 3 of Spades!

------

## Sample Turn (for clarity)

- Player leads suit. All must follow or use trump. Highest trump wins. First appearance of the teammate card triggers partnership reveal.

------

## Tips for Strategic Play

- Only bid high if you have strong scoring cards and potential teammates.
- Save trump cards for key moments.
- Track which suits are exhausted among opponents.
- Don’t reveal your partnership prematurely unless strategically critical.
